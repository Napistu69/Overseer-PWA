The Heroin-to-Aspirin Origin Story

In 1898, Bayer chemist Felix Hoffmann synthesized diacetylmorphine. Bayer trademarked it as “Heroin” — from the German “heroisch” (heroic), because test subjects reported feeling heroic.

The marketing was aggressive:

=> Marketed as a cough suppressant for children and adults => Promoted as a non-addictive alternative to morphine => Sold freely in pharmacies without prescription → Distributed through mail-order catalogs => Recommended by doctors for respiratory ailments

The reality: heroin is approximately 2-3 times more potent than morphine and equally addictive. Within a decade, the addiction epidemic was undeniable. Bayer stopped producing heroin in 1913 — not because of moral awakening, but because the Harrison Narcotics Tax Act (1914) restricted narcotics distribution.

The harm phase lasted decades. The profit phase continues: methadone clinics, rehabilitation programs, pharmaceutical interventions for opioid addiction — all generating revenue from treating the condition that heroin created.

The Aspirin Parallel

One year after heroin (1899), Bayer released aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid). The timing is not coincidental. The same laboratory, the same chemist, the same company. One substance for suppression, one for relief. Both patented. Both profitable.

Aspirin’s harm profile is less dramatic than heroin’s, but significant:

=> Gastrointestinal bleeding with chronic use => Reyes syndrome in children (associated with viral infection treatment) => Kidney impairment with long-term use => Interaction with blood thinners and anticoagulants

The “daily aspirin” regimen, promoted for decades as cardiovascular protection, was based on studies later revealed to have methodological flaws. The 2018 ARRIVE trial showed no significant benefit for primary prevention in low-risk populations, and guidelines were revised to recommend against routine aspirin use for prevention.

But the profit had already been extracted. Decades of daily consumption generated revenue. The harm (GI bleeding, bleeding complications) generated additional pharmaceutical interventions (proton pump inhibitors, antacid medications). Promise => Harm => Profit.

The Irony

Bayer introduced heroin as safe. Bayer introduced aspirin as a wonder drug. Bayer was wrong on both counts — or right, depending on whether one measures success by public health or by profit. The substances that caused harm also created the markets for treating that harm. The Black Lotus blooms from its own damage.
