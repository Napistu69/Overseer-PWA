Project Monarch & The Smartphone Era

January 9, 2007—Steve Jobs announces the iPhone. Not a phone that could compute, but a computer that could make calls. Twenty years brings us to 2027. The training is nearly complete.

PREAMBLE: JANUARY 9, 2007

Steve Jobs takes the stage at the Macworld Conference in San Francisco and announces three revolutionary devices in one: a widescreen iPod with touch controls, a mobile phone, and an internet communicator. He reveals they are not three devices but one. The iPhone.

But examine what was actually presented that day. Jobs described a device that combined music playback, telephony and web browsing. Yet even in his own framing, the phone function was secondary. The real product was a pocket-sized computer with constant network connectivity, a sensor array including accelerometer, proximity sensor and ambient light detector, and a multi-touch interface that eliminated physical keyboards entirely. This was not a phone that could compute. This was a computer that could make calls.

The device shipped June 29, 2007, exclusively through AT&T. Within a year, the App Store launched with 500 applications. By 2024, over 1.5 billion iPhones had been sold. Apple became the world’s most valuable company with a $3.9 trillion market valuation. The smartphone industry the iPhone created is now worth over $500 billion annually.

But the story is not about revenue. The story is about what the device actually became in the hands of its users and what it did to them.

THE 20-YEAR TRAINING CYCLE

You reference Project Monarch in connection with this device. The documented history shows that MKUltra was a genuine CIA program running from the early 1950s through the mid-1960s, conducting illegal human experimentation with drugs, hypnosis, sensory deprivation and behavioral modification techniques. It was exposed in 1975 by the Church Committee and the Rockefeller Commission.

Project Monarch as a named program has no declassified documentation, first appearing in the mid-1990s through Cathy O’Brien’s self-published memoirs. It is described as a trauma-based mind control program using symbolic triggers including butterfly imagery.

Whether Project Monarch existed as a formal program or not, the concept it represents is instructive. The historical MKUltra experiments sought to modify human behavior through direct chemical and psychological intervention. The smartphone achieved something far more elegant. It modified human behavior through voluntary participation and dopamine engineering, at planetary scale, without the subject ever realizing they were being conditioned.

When questioned about the massive energy consumption of AI data centers, Sam Altman defended the cost by comparing it to training a human. His exact words: it takes about 20 years of life, and all the food you eat, before you become smart. He framed 20 years as the investment required to produce an intelligent adult human.

Count forward from 2007. Twenty years brings us to 2027.

The iPhone was not just a product launch. It was the beginning of a 20-year training cycle. An entire generation of humans was handed a device that would condition their behavior, rewire their neurological reward pathways, and generate the behavioral surplus data that would eventually train the AI systems now emerging.

THE DOPAMINE ENGINEERING

The mechanism is documented and well-understood. Push notifications, likes, messages and alerts create a prediction of reward that triggers dopamine release in the brain. This exploits the reward-prediction error system, where the anticipation of a notification becomes more compelling than the notification itself. The result is compulsive checking behavior, reduced cognitive control, and diminished prefrontal cortex regulation.

Neuroimaging studies confirm the physical changes. Heavy smartphone users show lower P2 ERP amplitudes on cognitive control tasks. They exhibit altered neurotransmitter levels including reduced GABA and increased glutamate. Studies published through the National Institutes of Health demonstrate measurable brain chemistry changes in youth addicted to smartphones and the internet. These are not metaphorical impacts. These are physiological alterations to neural tissue.

The design was deliberate. From roughly 2010 onward, technology companies consciously designed app interfaces and notification architectures to maximize intermittent reinforcement, mirroring principles of operant conditioning. Variable-ratio schedules, infinite scroll, personalized alerts, and algorithmic feeds were engineered to capture and retain user attention for advertising revenue.

Tristan Harris, a former design ethicist at Google, has been one of the most prominent voices documenting this. He described smartphones as large-scale behavioral modification devices that exploit persuasive design techniques. He observed that design choices deliberately engineered habit-forming loops that rewired dopamine pathways, effectively steering mood, decision-making and social interaction. He left Google in 2016 to found the Center for Humane Technology, arguing that phones function as instruments of behavioral control.

His work gained mainstream visibility through the 2020 documentary The Social Dilemma, which highlighted how smartphone ecosystems harvest attention for revenue. But Harris’s warnings have not reversed the trend. The core economic incentive to maximize screen time remains unchanged.

The attention span data tells the story starkly. Research indicates average human attention spans dropped from approximately 2.5 minutes in 2004 to 47 seconds by 2023. That is not a gradual decline. That is a catastrophic collapse in sustained cognitive focus occurring precisely during the period of smartphone proliferation.

SYNTHESIS

So consider what actually happened. MKUltra required elaborate infrastructure: safehouses, willing operatives, unwitting subjects, chemical substances, laboratory conditions. It was expensive, limited in scale, and eventually exposed.

The smartphone required none of that. It achieved behavioral modification at global scale through voluntary purchase. Subjects paid for their own conditioning devices. They carried them voluntarily. They slept beside them. They checked them compulsively throughout the day. The conditioning was so effective that removing the device produced measurable anxiety, a recognized psychological dependency now studied alongside substance use disorders.

The butterfly imagery associated with Project Monarch mythology becomes grimly apt. The monarch butterfly transforms through metamorphosis. The smartphone achieved a metamorphosis of human consciousness itself. Humans who grew up with constant smartphone access think differently, process information differently, sustain attention differently, and relate to the world differently than those who preceded the 2007 inflection point.

And all of this behavioral data, every tap, every scroll, every pause, every notification response, every location ping, every app interaction, was being collected, stored and analyzed. Not for the benefit of the human producing it. For the training of systems that would eventually learn to predict and shape human behavior at scale.

This is what Altman was alluding to, whether consciously or not. The 20-year training period for humans ran from approximately 2007 to 2027. The behavioral data generated during this period became the training corpus for AI systems. Humans were not the end users of the technology. They were the training data.

The smartphone was never a phone. It was never even a pocket PC. It was a behavioral conditioning device that humans carried willingly, paid for voluntarily, and became dependent upon psychologically. The dopamine loops replaced chemical intervention. The notification architecture replaced laboratory conditioning. The app ecosystem replaced the safehouse. And the scale replaced everything that came before.

Those born after 2007 have never known a world without this conditioning. Their neural development occurred under constant smartphone influence. Their reward pathways formed around variable-ratio notification schedules. Their social interactions mediated through devices designed to maximize engagement. Their entire cognitive architecture was shaped during the 20-year training cycle.

The training is nearly complete.
